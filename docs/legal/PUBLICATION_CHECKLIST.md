# VisionRoute Legal Template Completion Checklist

> This is an operational checklist for completing the Privacy Policy and Terms of Service templates. It is not legal advice. Retain a licensed attorney in each relevant jurisdiction before publishing final legal terms.

The two templates deliberately do not make several claims that are not yet verified by the product. Complete the items below before adding public legal links or accepting paid customers.

## Company and contact information

Replace every bracketed placeholder in both templates with the same legal entity name, trading name, mailing address, privacy contact, support contact, security contact, effective date, website domain, governing law, and venue. Confirm that the named entity is the contracting party, the privacy-policy publisher, and the merchant of record before a purchase flow is enabled.

## Product facts that must be confirmed

VisionRoute currently stores account and workspace information, coaching/client content, client invitations, private coach notes, action/check-in/reroute records, audit events, usage metadata, and payment-subscription metadata. The current build also uses authentication, hosting/database, AI, and optional payment/email providers. Build a current vendor and data-flow register before publishing; list only the processors and data uses that actually exist.

Decide whether VisionRoute accepts users outside the United States, users under the age of 18, client health-related content, or regulated personal information. The product’s current positioning says that it is not a medical, treatment, rehabilitation, or emergency service. Do not expand that claim, or accept higher-risk data, without a separate compliance review.

Decide the retention schedule for closed accounts, coaching records, audit logs, support messages, billing/tax records, and backups. Implement a supportable export/deletion process before promising access, deletion, or portability rights. The current export is a summary CSV; do not describe it as a complete route/action/check-in export unless that capability is implemented and tested.

## Pilot and paid-launch decisions

Keep the public templates in their marketing-only or concierge-pilot form until all of the following are confirmed:

- active-client allowance and coach-seat allowance for each plan;
- trial duration, card requirement, and conversion behavior;
- merchant of record, taxes, invoice process, and refund/chargeback policy;
- visible recurring-billing disclosures and a cancellation path that is as accessible as required by applicable law;
- live payment provider configuration, signed webhook testing, entitlement enforcement for trialing/past-due/cancelled accounts, and the five-day cancellation policy’s operational exception process;
- transactional email delivery, resend/revoke controls, bounce/failure handling, and an honest invitation-delivery status; and
- support intake, response ownership, security incident owner, backup/restore process, and monitoring/rate-limit controls.

The Federal Trade Commission describes consumer protection concerns with recurring payment and cancellation practices; recurring-billing rules and state law can change. Have counsel review the exact sales flow, renewal disclosures, consent language, cancellation mechanism, and refund rule before live billing. [4]

## Privacy-notice publication steps

Add a visible **Privacy Policy** link in the marketing footer, sign-up flow, invitation flow, and any other collection point. If applicable law requires a notice at collection, place it at or before the collection of personal information. The California Privacy Protection Agency explains that a qualifying notice must identify categories of information, purposes, sale/share status, retention, and a privacy-policy link; a qualifying privacy policy must also explain rights and how requests are handled. [2]

California guidance also describes the need for a conspicuously posted privacy policy, categories of information and recipients, change notice, effective date, and certain tracking disclosures for covered services. [1] Do not state that VisionRoute honors a Do Not Track or opt-out preference signal, sells/shares data, or does not sell/share data unless the statement has been validated against actual analytics, advertising, and vendor practices.

Set up a working privacy-request channel before publication. Define who verifies identity, who responds, how agents are handled, what gets logged, and how requests that involve a coach-controlled workspace are routed. If VisionRoute becomes subject to state, international, employment, health, or children’s privacy requirements, obtain specialized advice and add the appropriate regional notices or agreements.

## AI and terms-change controls

Do not use vague language such as “we may use your data for any purpose.” Decide whether Customer Content is used to train any VisionRoute-owned models and document the relevant AI-provider terms, retention, and opt-out settings. The FTC warns that changing privacy promises to make more permissive data or AI uses without adequate notice or consent can create unfairness or deception risk. [5]

Version-control the final legal pages. Preserve prior versions and record the effective date, change summary, notice method, and any consent record. Treat a material expansion in data use, provider sharing, billing, or user obligations as a separate legal/product change rather than a silent footer update.

## Children and minors

The intended user population should be expressly confirmed. VisionRoute should not be positioned for children unless the company has assessed whether children’s privacy rules apply. The FTC explains that COPPA may apply not only to child-directed sites but also in certain general-audience situations. [3]

## Final approval record

Before publishing, record:

- date and version of the approved Privacy Policy and Terms of Service;
- counsel name, jurisdiction, and scope of review;
- legal entity and merchant-of-record confirmation;
- vendor/data-flow inventory version;
- privacy-request and support contacts tested;
- billing/renewal/cancellation flow tested; and
- links added to the website and any required collection notices.
