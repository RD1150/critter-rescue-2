# Pok Pok — US price benchmark

**Observation date:** 2026-10-09
**Item:** Pok Pok | **Category:** calm open-ended play | **Rank:** 2

**Selected US benchmark:** **$9.99 per month** for the App Store item labeled “Pok Pok Monthly.” This is a recurring subscription price, not an upfront purchase price. The company’s own pages confirm that access is sold as monthly or annual subscriptions but do **not** publish dollar amounts, so the numeric benchmark comes from the current **US Apple App Store** listing. [1] [2]

A **7-day free trial** applies to both monthly and annual subscriptions. Unless cancelled during the trial, the selected subscription begins automatically and is charged; Apple says it auto-renews unless turned off. The $9.99 monthly amount is therefore the post-trial monthly benchmark, not a promotional/trial price. [1] [2]

**Confidence: Medium.** Apple’s current US listing exposes multiple Pok Pok monthly/annual in-app-purchase entries, while neither the company FAQ nor the public checkout gives a dollar price or identifies the default checkout tier. This memo deliberately records only the clearly displayed $9.99 monthly entry as one current US benchmark; it does not infer that it is the only available plan or claim an annual price. The app remains active: Apple shows a version updated four days before observation. [2]

## Access-model comparison

Pok Pok is a subscription-funded, continually updated digital playroom for ages **2–7** (Apple describes 2–8). A single subscription unlocks its toys and updates, can be shared across family devices, and is ad-free. It works offline after the app is downloaded and a subscription is completed, although Pok Pok asks users to reconnect every few weeks so updates can load. [1] [2]

Against the described **calm, ad-free, local-first preschool rescue game for ages 3–6**, Pok Pok is comparable on calm/low-stimulation, ad-free child use and offline play after setup. Its access model is different in emphasis: it is a recurring subscription to an expanding, open-ended toy collection, rather than a discrete rescue-game experience. Pok Pok’s periodic connection for updates also makes it **offline-capable after activation**, not unequivocally local-first. No Critter Rescue payment model was supplied, so no claim is made that its price or purchase mechanics differ.
