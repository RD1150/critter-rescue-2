# Pok Pok — comparable evidence memo

**Comparable record.** Pok Pok is an active, direct positioning comparator: a low-stimulation Montessori digital playroom marketed to children ages **2–8**, which includes Critter Rescue’s proposed **3–6** audience. Its experience is deliberately open-ended rather than a linear game loop, and it is sold through recurring subscription access. The evidence below was observed **2026-09-26** from Pok Pok’s official pages and the current U.S. Apple App Store and Google Play listings. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

## Comparison test

|---|---|---|
| 1. Preschool-targeted play | **Yes** | Pok Pok’s homepage calls it “non-addictive, educational games for ages 2–8.” Its FAQ says it is designed especially for children 2–7. The U.S. App Store also describes it as “Toddler Learning Games 2–8+” and marks it “Made for Ages 0–5.” Ages 3–6 are therefore squarely within the stated audience, though the product spans beyond preschool. [1] [2] [4] |

## Product and commercial evidence

Pok Pok is not merely a single educational activity. It presents a continually expanding “playroom” of child-led digital toys, with “new toys added regularly.” This breadth is relevant to retention: the stated monetization rationale is continuing to add activities and updates, rather than charging for individual child actions. [1] [4]

**Mutable U.S. price evidence (observed 2026-09-26):** The current U.S. Apple listing was free to download with in-app purchases and listed multiple visible subscription SKUs: monthly **$6.99, $9.99, and $12.99**; annual **$45.99, $59.99, $69.99, and $79.99**; and a **$89.99 lifetime** purchase. The listing does not explain which monthly/annual amount applies to a newly selecting U.S. customer, or whether the entries represent legacy, regional, promotional, or plan variants. Accordingly, the **subscription model and displayed price points are verified**, but a single current default price is **not verified without completing a purchase**, which was not done. The official FAQ confirms monthly/annual subscriptions and the free-trial mechanics but does not publish dollar prices. [2] [4]

There is a presentation distinction worth preserving: Google Play labels the app “In-app purchases” / “In-Game Purchases,” while the developer’s copy in the same listing says “No in-app purchases” and later describes a subscription. The available evidence supports a paid subscription acquired through the storefront, but does **not** establish a child-facing à-la-carte purchase loop within play. The FAQ says children see no in-app purchase and parents manage subscription from the Grown-Ups Area. [2] [5]

## Safety and privacy comparison to Critter Rescue

Pok Pok is a strong **positioning** comparator because it combines “calm,” “non-addictive,” open-ended, Montessori-inspired play with explicit parent-facing assurances: no child-directed advertising, no child purchase prompts, COPPA positioning, offline play, a Grown-Ups/Parental Area, and family subscription sharing. [1] [2] [5]

Critter Rescue’s proposed privacy design would be materially more restrictive if implemented as described: **no ads, tracking, child accounts/profiles, social features, or child-facing commerce; local-only progress; parent controls behind a math gate.** Pok Pok’s published policy instead permits collection of device information and anonymous usage information; it offers optional parent accounts / multi-device login and stores some subscription-related information, while its store privacy disclosures identify purchases and other data categories. Pok Pok’s no-child-personal-information commitment remains relevant and credible as a safety positioning benchmark, but it is not equivalent to a zero-data/fully local-only model. [2] [3] [4] [5]

The child experience is also adjacent rather than identical. Pok Pok’s cited activity set is a wide 2D, hand-crafted digital toy collection covering creative, pretend, STEM, music, and discovery play. Critter Rescue’s 3D animal-care loop and gentle learning mini-games could form a distinct thematic proposition, but the research does **not** verify that prospective parents would pay for that difference. It also does not verify Critter Rescue’s signed-device performance or launch readiness.

## Viability signal and lessons

**Viability signal: positive category signal, not product validation.** A current, regularly updated app with a free trial, recurring subscriptions, broad repeatable play, app-store distribution, and explicit calm/privacy positioning demonstrates that this bundle of attributes can be commercially offered to the target-parent market. Pok Pok’s presence therefore supports treating Critter Rescue as a genuine direct-positioning category, not an untested category concept. It does not establish Critter Rescue’s price acceptance, conversion, retention, safety implementation quality, or device compatibility.

Key implications for Critter Rescue:

- **The calm/open-ended + repeatable-activity bundle is already established.** A single animal-care loop will likely need sufficient depth, variation, and a clear mini-game cadence to meet the repeat-play benchmark set by a growing multi-toy playroom. [1] [4]
- **Privacy claims are a core purchase-context signal, but precision matters.** Pok Pok pairs high-level “safe” and COPPA claims with detailed disclosures. Critter Rescue should ensure app-store labels, privacy policy, parent messaging, and actual SDK/data behavior all support its stronger local-only/no-tracking promise before launch. [3] [4] [5]
- **Parent separation should be explicit and testable.** Pok Pok discloses a locked Grown-Ups Area but the reviewed materials do not state the gate method. Critter Rescue can differentiate by explaining its math gate plainly and confirming it keeps settings and commerce away from child play.
- **A live subscription offer is not a requirement.** Pok Pok offers subscription access and a 7-day trial as one monetization route. The evidence does not show which of its multiple displayed U.S. price points is default, nor does it show that the same model will fit Critter Rescue before device testing and launch. [2] [4]

## Confidence notes

**High confidence** that Pok Pok satisfies all five comparison criteria: its own product, FAQ, privacy, and two current major-store pages directly cover audience, experience design, activity breadth, safety positioning/controls, and subscription monetization. **Moderate confidence** in exact price interpretation: the U.S. Apple listing displayed several active-looking monthly and annual amounts but did not identify their eligibility or default. **Not verified:** a Pok Pok math-gate mechanism, a single default U.S. subscription price, absence of all child social/sharing features, or parent demand/conversion for Critter Rescue. No purchase, signup, contact, or publication was undertaken.
