# Critter Rescue — Plan for the First 1,000 Paying Families

**Prepared:** October 9, 2026
**Author:** Manus AI

## Yes, $10,000 is possible—but 1,000 is a commercial milestone, not a guaranteed result

**RECOMMENDATION:** Treat **1,000 paying families** as the first serious revenue target for Critter Rescue if the base game is ultimately priced at **$9.99**. It is possible for a polished children’s game, but it will not happen merely because the game is completed. It requires a tested product, trusted parent-facing presentation, a clear store page, and repeated outreach to adults who choose apps for young children.

The word **families** matters. Children should never be the customer or the marketing target. The buyer and decision-maker is the parent or caregiver.

At $9.99, exactly 1,000 base-game purchases produce **$9,990.00 gross**. That is very close to the $10,000 gross-sales goal. It is not $10,000 of profit or take-home cash. After a 15% platform-fee sensitivity, 1,000 purchases leave **$8,491.50** before refunds, taxes, hosting, support, marketing, and content costs. After a 30% fee sensitivity, they leave **$6,993.00** before those costs.

| Goal definition | $9.99 base-game buyers needed | Why it is different |
|---|---:|---|
| $10,000 gross store sales | 1,002 | Gross sales ignore platform fees and every operating cost. |
| $10,000 after a 15% platform-fee sensitivity | 1,178 | This still excludes refunds, tax, support, marketing, and all other costs. |
| $10,000 after a 30% platform-fee sensitivity | 1,431 | This is the conservative platform-fee sensitivity before other costs. |

The current formal launch plan still records **$4.99** as the initial test price. If $9.99 is adopted, revise that price decision before the first Android release, because the paid/free configuration must be deliberate from the first testing sequence. [1]

## Do not try to find 1,000 families all at once

The right path is to prove one small step at a time. Each step answers a different question.

### Step 1: Prove that 15 families enjoy it

First complete the signed iOS and Android test-build work and the real-device smoke test. Then use the existing free, caregiver-led beta with **10–15 households**. This is not a sales campaign. The purpose is to confirm that a child can choose an activity, that the parent gate stays adult-only, that local progress works, and that adults understand the game’s calm value. The current beta packet already defines the privacy limits: recruit caregivers, do not request child identifiers, and do not reuse tester contact information as a marketing list. [2]

**Continue only if:** there are no critical child-flow, privacy, parental-gate, or reliability failures, and adults say the game is clear enough to consider purchasing.

### Step 2: Earn the first 25 paid families

After the free beta is stable, release one paid version to a small adult audience. The target is **25 settled, non-refunded purchases**, not social-media likes, downloads, or survey promises. Ask each adult buyer one short question: “What made this feel worth buying for your family?” The answers reveal whether the lead message should be plushie rescue, calm play, early learning, accessibility, or privacy-respectful local progress.

**Continue only if:** families use the game after the first session, refunds are low, and the value explanation is consistent enough to use in the store listing.

### Step 3: Reach 100 families through trusted adult channels

Do not buy broad ads first. Start with places where an adult already wants ideas for calm preschool play:

- parent friends and adult referrals;
- local parenting groups only where the group rules allow a clearly identified creator post;
- early-childhood educators, librarians, and family-play creators who reach caregivers;
- small parent-oriented creators who can show their own adult perspective without collecting, showing, or profiling children.

Give each channel a distinct store-page link or campaign label. That makes it possible to see whether a creator, a parent group, or organic store search produces actual buyers. Do not pay for testimonials, require a positive review, or use a child’s image, voice, name, or personal story in promotion.

**Continue only if:** at least one channel produces real purchases at a cost that does not consume the margin from a $9.99 sale.

### Step 4: Make the store page do the selling

For a paid children’s game, the store page must persuade the parent before the child enters the app. The first screenshots and preview should communicate three promises within seconds:

1. **A calm plushie rescue world** — show the recognizable animal characters and one clear action.
2. **Meaningful play without pressure** — show a gentle learning or care activity without scores, timers, ads, or a noisy interface.
3. **Adult peace of mind** — state the real boundaries accurately: no child accounts, no child-facing purchase prompts, local progress, and adult-only settings. Do not claim “fully offline,” “COPPA certified,” or “no data collected.”

Apple allows product-page testing of alternate icons, screenshots, and preview videos. It can compare up to three alternatives against the original and reports impressions, conversion rate, and confidence in App Analytics. [3] Google Play offers store-listing experiments for graphics and localized text, with acquisition and one-day retention metrics. [4] Run one clear test at a time—for example, “plushie rescue” first screenshot versus “calm early learning” first screenshot—rather than changing every asset at once.

**Continue only if:** one message demonstrably converts better than the alternative. Keep the winner and test the next most important message.

### Step 5: Scale from 100 to 1,000 families only after one channel works

The following is a **working allocation**, not a forecast. It prevents the mistake of expecting one video or one listing to create all 1,000 purchases.

| Potential source | Working target | What is being tested | Decision rule |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Adult referrals and local parent communities | 150 purchases | Does the calm plushie concept earn word-of-mouth among known adults? | Continue only where group rules permit the post and purchases follow. |
| Parent-oriented creators | 250 purchases | Can adults who already discuss preschool play explain the value credibly? | Keep only creators whose tracked links produce purchases or qualified beta interest. |
| Organic App Store and Google Play discovery | 300 purchases | Can the store page turn parent search interest into paid installs? | Use store-page tests and keep the best-performing message. [3] [4] |
| Educator, librarian, and family-play community outreach | 150 purchases | Does adult professional interest create credible referrals without making educational claims? | Continue only after clear adult interest and zero child-data collection. |
| Seasonal updates, press interest, and repeat word-of-mouth | 150 purchases | Does a timely, genuine game update bring new adult attention? | Use only accurate release news; do not create artificial urgency. |
| **Total working target** | **1,000 purchases** | Each channel must be measured separately. | Shift effort to channels that produce settled purchases. |

The allocation is intentionally diversified. No part claims that a particular channel will produce those exact counts. The purpose is to test several adult acquisition paths and concentrate only on the ones that work.

## A practical 90-day sequence after the release gate

**Days 1–30:** Complete the signed-device and caregiver beta gates. Fix any blocking problems. Create the parent-facing store page, accurate screenshots, and a short device-captured preview. Do not collect child information and do not market to beta contacts without separate adult opt-in.

**Days 31–60:** Run the small paid test. Seek the first 25 settled purchases. Track only the minimum adult-level, channel-level data needed to understand which outreach path produced the purchase. Compare at least two store-page messages using the platform’s native testing tools where traffic supports a meaningful result. [3] [4]

**Days 61–90:** Expand the one or two strongest adult channels. Aim for 100 total paid families, a stable low-refund experience, and a documented answer to “why did parents buy?” Do not scale paid promotion or commit to rapid expansion modules until this evidence exists.

## The one number to watch each week

Track **settled, non-refunded paid families by acquisition channel**. That is more useful than likes, views, followers, app-page impressions, or free-test signups.

A simple private operator tracker should have only these fields: week, adult acquisition channel, paid families, refunded purchases, known marketing spend, and a non-identifying note on the parent value message. Do not add child names, ages, behavioral profiles, or individual play histories.

## What has to be true before this plan starts

The game still requires the external release tasks already documented in the project: final publisher identity and monitored adult support contact, completed rights register and vendor operations, signed native iOS/Android builds, real-device online and airplane-mode testing, and correct Apple/Google declarations. The code is not yet a signed commercial binary. [1] [2]

This plan does **not** publish the app, contact creators, spend money, enroll any account, or change the current pricing decision. It gives a measured path to validate whether 1,000 paying families is realistic.
