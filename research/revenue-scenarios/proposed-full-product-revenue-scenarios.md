# Critter Rescue — Proposed Full-Product Revenue Scenarios

**Prepared:** October 9, 2026
**Author:** Manus AI

## Direct answer

Under the **proposed, not yet adopted** commercial structure—**$9.99** for the base game and **$0.99** for each permanent expansion module—Critter Rescue could produce anything from a small validation result to a meaningful independent-game business. The model does **not** support a single reliable number today because Critter Rescue has no paid sales, install volume, acquisition-cost, refund, retention, or expansion-purchase data. [4]

In the first 12 months, the scenario range below is **$2,621.25 to $697,500.00 gross revenue**, before platform fees. The middle “small viable niche” case—2,500 base-game buyers who buy an average of two modules—produces **$29,925.00 gross** and **$20,947.50–$25,436.25** after a 30%–15% platform-fee sensitivity, before every other cost.

### What those scenarios mean

**Early paid test — 250 buyers.** This produces **$2,621.25 gross**. It is a good sign that adult buyers exist, but it is not proof that the product can fund regular module creation.

**Small viable niche — 2,500 buyers.** This produces **$29,925.00 gross**. This is the first scenario that could become a meaningful small business, but only if actual acquisition cost, content-production labor, refunds, and support are modest enough to leave positive contribution.

**Strong indie success — 15,000 buyers.** This produces **$194,400.00 gross**. That outcome requires more than a completed app: it requires a polished store listing, trusted parent positioning, reliable releases, good reviews, and repeatable discovery through channels that do not make child-data or advertising promises unsafe.

**Exceptional breakout — 50,000 buyers.** This produces **$697,500.00 gross**. It is possible, but it should not be used as a budget or hiring plan. Treat it as an upside case to revisit only after the game has actual organic demand and low refund rates.

## The practical interpretation

**RECOMMENDATION:** Aim first for the **small viable niche** case, not the breakout case. A clear early commercial milestone would be 25 settled adult payments after the stated refund window, followed by a beta that shows children return and parents buy at least one module without pressure. Only after that should paid acquisition, a larger content roadmap, or physical-product inventory be considered.

The $9.99-plus-$0.99 model can work because it is simple for parents: they own the base game, then choose whether each future module is worth buying. It is weaker if modules feel like small fragments removed from the original game. The base purchase therefore needs to stand on its own, and each paid module needs to feel like a genuine self-contained activity area with permanent access. Current preschool-app comparisons show that the closest large competitors use either recurring libraries or free entry with individual content packs, rather than proving a single best model for Critter Rescue. [3]

No child should see prices, locked-content messages, or urgency. If this model is adopted, new modules must be sold only in an adult area behind the existing parental gate, as permanent non-consumable purchases with restore-purchase support. [1] [2]

## What would change this forecast

The largest missing driver is **customer acquisition**: how many parent-qualified store-page viewers become buyers, and what it costs to bring each one there. The second is **module attachment**: the average number of expansions each base buyer purchases. The third is the real cost to produce one module. The current project has none of those observed values, so the scenarios are decision-support ranges, not a forecast of actual revenue.
